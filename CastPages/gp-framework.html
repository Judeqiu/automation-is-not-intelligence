The Big Picture
GP is not a
content subject.

GP tests your ability to think critically, construct arguments, and communicate clearly — not your ability to memorise facts.

The student who memorises 100 examples but cannot form a thesis will score a C. The student with 30 well-processed examples who argues with clarity and evaluation will score an A.

GP is a thinking subject disguised as a general knowledge subject. Treat it as the former and you will thrive. Treat it as the latter and you will always struggle.

The Exam Structure
Two Papers.
One Goal.

1 Paper 1: Essay

50% · 90 min

  • 8 questions → choose 1
  • Write 500–800 words
  • Present a structured, evidence-backed argument on an issue of global or local significance

2 Paper 2: Comprehension

50% · 90 min

  • 3 passages (~1200 words total)
  • SAQs + Comparison Questions NEW + Summary + AQ (12 marks)
  • Read precisely, paraphrase accurately, evaluate arguments, apply to Singapore
Paper 1 Deep Dive
The Essay:
90 minutes to argue.

8 questions spanning 6 topic areas. You pick one. You have 90 minutes to plan, write, and review an essay of 500–800 words.

The questions are not factual ("What is climate change?"). They are debatable propositions:

"No country should sacrifice its economic development in favour of preserving the environment." Discuss.

"Democracy is the perfect form of government." Discuss.

"Learning facts is no longer necessary because information can be instantly accessed online." Evaluate.

The examiner does not want you to list facts about the topic. They want your position, defended with evidence, evaluated critically.

Paper 2 Deep Dive
Comprehension:
Precision is everything.

3 passages (~1200 words total) presenting different viewpoints on the same topic. You have 90 minutes to answer all components.

SAQs (17 marks)

Literal, inference, language use, attitude, tone, punctuation questions. Answer formula: Answer → Evidence → Explain. Always paraphrase — never lift.

Comparison Qs NEW 8881

Cross-reference Passage C with A or B. Does C support or contradict? Identify → Quote → Justify. 4–6 marks.

Summary (8 marks)

Based on ALL of Passage 2. No opening phrase given. 80–120 words. Scan everything. Paraphrase everything. Count words.

AQ (12 marks)

Evaluate the author's arguments and apply them to Singapore. Use specific local examples. 12 marks = highest single question. Budget 25 min.

What the Examiner Marks
The 3 Assessment Objectives
Every mark in GP maps to one of these. AO3 is the discriminator.
AO1 · ~35%
Understand & Explain
Demonstrate knowledge of issues across disciplines. This is your content — necessary but not sufficient.
AO2 · ~35%
Apply & Construct
Use knowledge to build arguments. Select and deploy evidence to support a position. This is your structure.
AO3 · ~30%
Evaluate & Judge
Critically analyse claims. Present your own assessment. Engage with counterarguments. This is the A/C boundary — your independent thinking.

Students who stop at AO1 ("here is what I know") cap at C. AO3 is the difference.

⚠️ New Student Warning
The Content Trap

Most JC1 students believe: "If I just read more, I'll get better at GP."

This is wrong. Reading more without a processing system is like pouring water into a leaky bucket. Within 48 hours, you forget most of what you read. Within a week, it's gone.

✓ The Right Approach:

Read → Process (extract argument + evidence + your evaluation) → File into a knowledge bank organised by topic → Retrieve during practice. Every article you read must leave a trace in your bank. No trace = wasted time.

The Right Approach
Knowledge Bank
+ Frameworks
+ Deliberate Practice
Three pillars. All three must be in place. Missing any one guarantees a plateau.

📚 Knowledge Bank

A structured digital notebook organised by topic area. Every entry = argument + specific evidence + your evaluation. Build 80–100 entries over JC1. Your essay ammunition.

🔧 Frameworks

PEEL for paragraphs. REACT for AQ. Thesis formula for introductions. 9 SAQ answering formulas. Memorise these until they are automatic.

🎯 Deliberate Practice

Not mindless repetition. Every practice session targets a specific weakness identified from your error log. 2–3 essay outlines + 1 full paper per week. Practice what you're bad at, not what you're comfortable with.

The Essay Framework
Thesis-First
→ PEEL
→ Counterargument

Step 1: Thesis-First (10 min). Before you write a single word, state your position in one clear, debatable sentence. "While [counterpoint], I argue [position] because [reason 1, 2, 3]." No thesis = no essay.

Step 2: PEEL Paragraphs (3–4 × 12 min). Point (debatable topic sentence) → Evidence (specific: named, dated, quantified) → Explanation (WHY this supports your thesis — this is where AO3 lives) → Link (tie back to thesis).

Step 3: Counterargument + Rebuttal (1 para). "Admittedly, some argue that [strongest opposing view]..." Then rebut: "However, this argument overlooks [your rebuttal]..." An essay without a counterargument cannot score A.

Step 4: Conclusion. Synthesise (don't summarise). Connect your arguments into a larger insight. End with a forward-looking statement. No new evidence in the conclusion.

The Comprehension Framework
SAQs → Summary
→ Comparison
→ AQ

SAQs (17 marks) · 35 min

9 question types, each with a fixed answer formula. Answer → Evidence → Explain. Always paraphrase. Never lift. If stuck >3 min, circle and return.

Comparison Qs NEW · 4–6 marks

Does Passage C support or contradict A/B? Identify idea in C → Quote both passages → Justify the relationship. Say "supports" or "contradicts" explicitly.

Summary (8 marks) · 25 min

Based on ALL of Passage 2 (Syllabus 8881). Scan every paragraph. 80–120 words. 5-point check: requirements, location, degree, connotation, overarching point. No opening phrase provided — write your own.

AQ (12 marks) · 25–30 min

REACT framework: Restate author's argument → Evaluate → Apply to Singapore with specific examples → Contrast/complicate → Takeaway. 12 marks. Don't rush this.

The Long Game
JC1 → JC2
Progression

JC1 Term 1–2

Build the knowledge bank. Read daily, process into bank. Memorise all frameworks (PEEL, REACT, SAQ formulas). Practice essay outlines — no full essays yet. Goal: 40+ bank entries.

JC1 Term 3–4

Introduce timed full essays and Paper 2. Start error log. Identify your weakest topic areas and question types. Goal: 70+ bank entries. 15+ outlines.

JC2 → A-Levels

Timed full papers weekly. Targeted drills on weak areas. Consolidate bank (100+ entries). Review all outlines. Goal: Error-free execution under time pressure.

⚠️ Avoid These
Common Traps New
Students Fall Into
1

Reading without processing

Scrolling CNA every day but never extracting arguments or evidence. This is entertainment, not study.

2

Writing before thinking

Starting to write an essay without a thesis. The essay discovers its argument in paragraph 3. The examiner has already decided your grade by then.

3

Memorising model essays

GP questions are never identical. You are tested on your ability to construct an argument, not to reproduce one. Memorise evidence, not essays.

4

Neglecting Paper 2 until JC2

Paper 2 is 50% of your grade. The new Comparison Questions require specific practice. Start Paper 2 in JC1 Term 1. Do not wait.

5

Vague evidence

"Many countries have tried to reduce pollution..." No. Name the country. Name the policy. Give the year. Specificity = credibility = marks.

6

No counterargument

An essay without a counterargument cannot demonstrate AO3. You are telling the examiner: "I have not considered any perspective other than my own." This alone caps you at B.

Start Here
Your First Week
Action Plan

1 Day 1: Set up your knowledge bank

Create a Notion/Google Doc. Make one page per topic area (6 pages). This is your GP infrastructure. Everything you read from now on goes into this bank.

2 Day 2–3: Read & process 3 articles

Use the 3-Pass Method: Extract argument → Capture evidence → Write your evaluation. File each into the correct topic area. Congratulations — you now have your first 3 bank entries.

3 Day 4: Memorise the core frameworks

PEEL paragraph structure. Thesis formula ("While X, I argue Y because A, B, C."). REACT for AQ. 9 SAQ question types. Write them on a card. Keep it on your desk.

4 Day 5–6: Try your first essay outline

Pick a past-year question from a topic area where you now have bank entries. 30 minutes. Thesis + 3 topic sentences + evidence + counterargument. No full paragraphs yet. Just the skeleton.

5 Day 7: Review and set the rhythm

Review your bank. Read 1 more article. Schedule your recurring weekly slots (Mon/Wed read, Tue outline, Thu Paper 2, Fri AQ, Weekend full paper). The habit is built.
